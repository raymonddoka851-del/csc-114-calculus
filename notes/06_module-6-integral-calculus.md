3.1 Integration as the Inverse of Differentiation

In the previous topic, we have studied differentiating common functions through use of various techniques of differentiation. In this topic, we carry out the process of differentiation in reverse. That is, we start with a given function, say f(x), and ask what function or functions, F(x), would have f(x) as  their derivative. This leads us to the concepts of an antiderivative and integration.

Antiderivative – Differentiation in Reverse

Definition: A function F is called an antiderivative of f on an interval if F'x=f(x) for all x in that interval.

Result: If F is an antiderivative of f on an interval, then the most general antiderivative of f on that interval is Fx+C, where C is an arbitrary constant.

Definition: The set of all antiderivatives of the function f on an interval is called the indefinite integral of f and is denoted f(x)dx, read “the integral of fx, dx”.

Thus,

f(x)dx=Fx+C,

Where F is an antiderivative of f and C is an arbitrary constant called the constant of integration.

The symbol ∫is called the integral sign and f(x) is called the integrand.

The symbol dx has no official meaning by itself. Its purpose is to show which variable the integral is with respect to. Note that the integral sign must always be written with dx.

Definition: The process of finding an antiderivative is called integration.

Example

Find x7dx.

Solution

An antiderivative of x7 is 18x8.

Hence

x7dx=18x8+C.

Note that we can check this result by differentiating:

Fx=18x8+C⟹F'x=x7.

Note: The derivative of a constant (in this case C) is zero.

Basic Integration Formulae / Standard Forms

1dx=x+C.

kdx=kx+C, where k is any constant.

xndx=1n+1xn+1+C, for n≠-1.

x-1dx=1xdx=lnx+C.

exdx=ex+C.

ekxdx=1kekx+C, where k is any constant.

kf(x)dx=kf(x)dx, where k is any constant.

f(x)±g(x)dx=f(x)dx±g(x)dx.

3.2 Definite Integrals

The quantity abf(x)dx is called the definite integral of f(x) from a to b.

The number a and b are known as the lower and upper limits of the integral.

Evaluating Definite Integrals

Example 1

Find 14x2dx.

Solution

14x2dx=x33+C14=Evaluate at upper limit-Evaluate at lower limit

=433+C-133+C=643-13

=21.

Example 2

Find -23x3dx.

Solution

-23x3dx=x44-23=344--244=16.25.

Example 3

Find 0π/2cos(x)dx.

Solution

0π/2cos(x)dx=sin(x)0π/2=sinπ2-sin0=1-0=1.
